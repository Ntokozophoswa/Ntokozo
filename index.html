01 — Business & Systems AnalysisREQUIREMENTS

Challenge

Business needs must be understood consistently by stakeholders, analysts, developers, and QA before implementation begins.

My contribution

Documented business requirements and supported their translation into functional and non-functional requirements, user stories, and acceptance considerations. Kept the intended business outcome visible while helping teams understand what a requirement means in practice.

Methods and outputs

Requirements elicitation, user stories, functional specifications, gap analysis, as-is/to-be analysis, process mapping, UML, BPMN, and UAT coordination support.

AnalysisStakeholder communicationDocumentationFacilitation
02 — Technical Analysis & Solution DesignSYSTEMS

Challenge

Technical teams need enough context to make implementation decisions without losing the original business intent.

My contribution

Prepared technical, security, audit-trail, monitoring, recovery, validation, error-handling, timeout, deployment, and testing considerations. Helped explain data relationships and component dependencies, while weighing security, time, priority, integration impact, and maintainability.

Methods and outputs

Technical requirements, data-model overviews, ERDs, sequence and state-transition diagrams, validation rules, negative scenarios, edge cases, and API/integration considerations.

Systems thinkingRisk awarenessData relationshipsTechnical translation
03 — Agile Delivery & Product ImprovementITERATE

Context

A confidential messaging-based ordering solution is being developed iteratively. The portfolio generalizes the product and omits identifying details.

My contribution

Helped relay demo feedback to the Scrum Master/manager and delivery team. Supported a shift from focusing mainly on core functionality toward balancing functional reliability with a smoother, more intuitive user journey. Helped keep bugs and experience improvements visible before a client-facing demonstration.

Iteration loop

Demonstrate→Gather feedback→Prioritize fixes→Refine experience↻

This is an example of iterative delivery and continuous improvement—not a claim that every issue has already been resolved.

04 — Process Improvement & Delivery EnablementTRACEABILITY

Challenge

When requirements are scattered across pages and tickets, developers and QA can spend too much time locating the full context.

My contribution

Working on a more structured Jira and Confluence approach that brings related business, functional, technical, and security requirements together. The intended view uses concise descriptions, linked requirement identifiers, relevant design references, and connected Jira work items, with Requirements Yogi supporting traceability.

Illustrative workflow

Epic→Story / requirement→BA→SA→Development→QA

The precise hierarchy and handoffs depend on the team's configured workflow. This improvement is in progress, not presented as a completed transformation.
